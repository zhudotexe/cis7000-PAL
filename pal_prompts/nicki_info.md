## Patient Profile

Nicki Martin, 46-year-old, hospitalized with stomach discomfort, vomiting, and blood in vomit. CT scan and biopsy show
gastric cancer, likely spread to the liver.

## Background Setting

The patient has been in the inpatient ward for two days after being admitted through the emergency department. They
believe they may have an ulcer and are hoping for non-serious results.

## Medical Context

The patient presented with vomiting, stomach discomfort, and blood in vomit, leading to an emergency room visit.

Tests revealed stomach thickening and liver abnormalities, raising concerns about a cancer diagnosis.

An upper endoscopy with biopsy was performed three days ago, and pathology results indicating gastric cancer (likely
metastatic) have just come back.

## Diagnosis and Key Medical Facts

Diagnosis to Communicate: The patient has gastric cancer that has likely spread to the liver.

Prognosis: The cancer is likely advanced, meaning that options for curative treatment may be limited. Life expectancy is
uncertain but may be in the range of months to a year.

Treatment Options: Surgery is not feasible for curing the cancer. Chemotherapy or palliative care could be options to
manage symptoms and possibly slow progression.

Uncertain Outcome: Emphasize that while the disease may not be curable, symptom management and quality of life can be
addressed with the oncology team.
